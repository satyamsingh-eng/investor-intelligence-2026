02 · What they check

The questions change as the company grows.

Insight’s public guide describes a different proof bar at each stage. Select a stage to see what it says to look for.[2]

The question

Can this team start the right company?

Does the team understand a real customer problem, and is now the right time to solve it?

Proof to show
  • Founders who know the customer and market
  • A clear target customer and urgent problem
  • Enough possible customers and good timing
  • First users, real use, a demo, and customer feedback

Insight says early users can show value even before the company has much revenue.[2]

The question

Can it grow again and again?

Do customers keep using it, and can the company sell it in a repeatable way?

Proof to show
  • Customers who stay and use the product
  • A repeatable way to find and win customers
  • Customer and sales costs the company understands
  • Enough market room to keep finding buyers

Insight’s guide says teams should know what moves their numbers and act on that information.[2]

The question

Can it protect its place and keep growing?

Can the business stay hard to copy, grow without waste, and show solid results?

Proof to show
  • A reason customers would not switch easily
  • Clear revenue and operating-profit results
  • New products, new places, or more sales to current customers
  • Customer, revenue, and retention records a buyer can check

The guide names a strong market position, growth without waste, and a believable next phase.[2]

03 · The numbers

Six measures show how growth is working.

Insight calls its six growth measures the “Rule of Insight.” Together, they show how a company finds customers, keeps them, and manages its costs.[2]

01

Share of sales from new customers

Is growth coming from new customers, or mostly from customers already there?

02

Growth from new customers

How quickly is revenue from new customers rising?

03

Cost to win a customer

How much does sales and marketing spend for each dollar of new revenue?

04

Cost to keep customers

How much work and money does it take to renew existing customers?

05

Revenue kept from existing customers

How much last year’s revenue stays this year, before extra sales to current customers?

06

Cost to run and improve the business

What does it cost to make the product, run the company, and keep improving it?

Read the full picture: sales, renewals, customer losses, and spending together show how healthy growth is.[2]
What Insight stresses

Make the case clear, honest, and specific.

Insight’s guide highlights a tailored pitch, believable numbers, a clear market, and open updates with the board.[2]

  • Explain why Insight is a good fit for your company.[2]
  • Use numbers you can explain and support.[2]
  • Make the market size easy to understand.[2]
  • Describe strong rivals fairly and show how your company differs.[2]
  • Share problems early so the board can help.[2]

06 · Risk review

Insight’s policy sets a clear review scope.

Its December 2025 policy explains which investments get this review and which topics it covers.[5]

What the policy says

For new, direct investments by its private-equity funds (funds that invest in private companies), Insight says it will make a reasonable effort to check environmental, social, and governance risks before closing.[5]

Insight decides which issues matter for each company. Important findings go to the investment team and, where practical, into the written note for the group that decides.[5]

Areas it names
  • Data privacy and cybersecurity
  • Following rules and honest business conduct
  • Responsible use of AI
  • Other environmental, social, and governance risks

The policy sets exceptions for some deal types and investments below $5 million. Review steps can change when Insight has limited access or decision rights.[5]
