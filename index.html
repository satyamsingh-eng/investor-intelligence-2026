03 — Track B

MIT track — verified with explicit gates

The MIT track carries harder eligibility constraints than Cornell. Three of the nine firms require an MIT-affiliated founder, which C3A does not have. Those are listed as gated, not as targets.

Binding constraint

MIT affiliation is the gate, not the asset

E14 Fund states a particular interest in MIT alumni and focuses on climate tech, advanced materials, and deep tech. SBXi states outright that it invests exclusively in founders who graduated from MIT. E62 invests in MIT-affiliated startups. C3A has no known MIT-graduate founder.

Action: approach the MIT track as an ecosystem and capital route with explicit eligibility caveats, not as a guaranteed fit. The three open routes that do not require an MIT founder are MileMark (Sloan alumni network), Tectonic (applied-AI spinouts), and Alumni Ventures Castor (co-investment).

05 — Method and evidence

How every claim is backed

University affiliation, current role, firm stage, AI thesis, and contactability are five separate evidence tracks. Apollo corroborates identity and contact only — it never proves affiliation, authority, thesis, or check size.

Evidence separation standard
Evidence track What counts as proof What is never proof
University affiliation Official university page, or fund page naming the institution Apollo record, LinkedIn profile, aggregator listing
Current role Official firm team page Prior fund bio, press mention, LinkedIn headline
Firm stage Official fund page stating check size or stage focus Crunchbase stage tag, third-party investor directory
AI thesis Official thesis page, or named AI portfolio companies Sector tag, self-described AI investor
Contactability Apollo work email with verified status, plus LinkedIn Pattern-guessed email, personal email, phone number
Warm intro Named program, board, class, or application path Shared alumni network assumed to equal access
